Research

The institute

An international energy research institute

Transition Economics Institute publishes careful, sourced analysis of energy markets and the economics of transition. We ask whether policy, contracts, and investment actually add up on the balance sheet, not only in the press release.

Energy transitions are financial and operational problems as much as they are engineering problems. Tariffs must recover cost. Capacity payments must match the demand that shows up on the system. LNG contracts must clear in the destinations that matter.

Coverage

What we cover

Three linked desks. Pakistan work focuses on power-sector reform, circular debt, gas allocation, and planning tools such as the IGCEP. United States work examines wholesale markets, large loads, interconnection, LNG, and federal and state regulation. Global work follows oil and gas balances, LNG trade, shipping chokepoints, and the coal and minerals systems that sit behind the energy transition.

  • Pakistan Desk Power-sector reform, circular debt, tariffs, gas allocation and generation planning.
  • United States Desk Wholesale markets, large loads, interconnection, LNG exports and federal and state regulation.
  • China Desk Power demand, coal and renewables build, grid investment, LNG buying and clean-technology exports.
  • India Desk Peak demand, coal supply, renewable auctions, transmission, distribution finance and fuel imports.
  • Middle East Desk Gulf supply, OPEC+ policy, Hormuz and Red Sea shipping, and regional gas and power.
  • Europe Desk Gas storage and LNG, power prices, market design, carbon pricing and the renewables build.
  • Africa Desk Electricity access, utility reform, South African load-shedding, gas projects and mini-grids.
  • Global Desk Oil and gas balances, LNG trade, coal, critical minerals and international energy finance.
  • Other Markets Occasional pieces on Latin America, Southeast Asia, Australia, Central Asia and elsewhere.

Approach

How the work is framed

We write for practitioners who need the cash-flow and contract view.

Each piece starts from published evidence: regulatory filings, planning documents, trade data, and market reports. The aim is to make the transition readable in operational and financial terms, so that readers can see where the numbers close and where they do not.
